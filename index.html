Launch it, then gravity does the rest


A projectile is any object that moves under gravity alone after it is launched. Its path is a smooth curve called a parabola.


Green arrow: horizontal velocity. Blue arrow: vertical velocity. Orange arrow: the actual velocity. Watch the blue one shrink, flip and grow.

Two motions that never interfere

Drop one ball and fire another sideways at the same instant. They stay level all the way down, so sideways speed has no effect on the fall.

Horizontal

No force, so acceleration is zero and speed is constant.

Vertical

Gravity pulls down at g ≈ 9.8 m/s², so speed changes steadily.

Split the launch velocity

One range, two angles

Angles that add up to 90° land in the same spot. The peak of the curve, at 45°, is the longest throw.

Where you see it, and what to remember

Sports

Basketball shots, javelin and football kicks all follow parabolas.

Engineering

Fountains, fireworks and water jets are designed with these equations.

Space and safety

Ballistics and landing-zone planning depend on projectile paths.

  • Horizontal and vertical motion are independent.
  • Horizontal velocity is constant; vertical acceleration is g downward.
  • At the top, vy = 0, but the projectile is still moving sideways.
  • Real air resistance shortens the range and makes the path asymmetric.
